Sharper colors, lower delay, sound on the PC, and controls on the phone while connected.

### Lower delay

- the phone holds a low-latency Wi-Fi lock while mirroring, so Wi-Fi power saving no longer adds delay
- video packets are marked for low delay (DSCP EF) and both sides use bigger socket buffers
- the phone encoder runs at H.264 level 4.2 with realtime priority and no per-frame allocations. Keyframes come every 10 s or right away when the desktop asks, instead of every second, so there are no regular bursts
- the desktop decodes a frame the moment it arrives instead of polling
- adaptive bitrate steps down gently on loss or a delay spike (2 Mbps floor) and climbs back after 3 s of a clean link
- `krapy connect -v` now splits the delay into phone, network and decode

### Controls on the phone

- while connected the app shows a control panel: Sound on PC, pause and resume, Smooth / Balanced / Sharp quality, keep the screen awake, clipboard sync, rotate the PC view, disconnect, and live fps, delay and bitrate
- pause, quality and disconnect are also in the notification
- Sound on PC sends the phone audio to the computer (Android 10+, apps that allow capture). Turn it off to keep sound on the phone. `krapy connect --no-audio` turns it off on the PC side
- `krapy connect --usb --show-touches` shows taps on the phone screen and puts the setting back when you quit
- Ctrl+R rotates the view on the PC
- turning the phone screen off while mirroring is not possible for a normal app, so it is not included

### Colors and quality

- the picture looked washed out because the color was converted for the screen twice. Fixed, and the desktop now uses the color range and matrix the phone sends
- sharper scaling in both directions, and the window opens at the phone shape
- new defaults: 12 Mbps, 60 fps, up to 1920. Sharp is 20 Mbps up to 2560, Smooth is 6 Mbps up to 1280

### USB: Start now not showing

- `krapy connect --usb` wakes the phone, waits for you to unlock it, and opens krapy fresh every time so the Start now dialog shows
- the phone only asks for capture once the app is on screen, and if the dialog does not come up or you cancel it there is a Start button in the app

On a loopback test (60 fps 720p clip, send to decoded) the median is 4 to 9 ms over UDP and 3 to 6 ms over TCP. Real glass-to-glass time depends on the phone encoder and the Wi-Fi.

### USB in short

1. `sudo dnf install android-tools` (Fedora) or `sudo apt install adb`
2. On the phone: Settings > About phone > tap Build number 7 times, then Developer options > USB debugging on
3. Plug in the phone and tap Allow
4. `krapy connect --usb`, unlock the phone, then tap Start now (or Start in the app)
