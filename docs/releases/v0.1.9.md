Sharper picture, steadier delay in fast games, click and drag from the PC, and no more false "unlock the phone" over USB.

### Picture

- the phone now sends its native size when its encoder can do it, instead of scaling down to 1920. Balanced is 16 Mbps (up to 2400 on the long side), Sharp is 24 Mbps at native size, Smooth is 8 Mbps up to 1280
- H.264 High profile with no frame reordering when the phone supports it (better detail for the same bitrate), with the right H.264 level for the size. If a phone reorders frames anyway, krapy switches back to the old profile by itself
- the PC samples colour the way H.264 stores it, so thin coloured edges look cleaner
- adaptive bitrate is calmer: it only steps down on real loss and never drops below half the preset

### Fast games

- the encoder is capped at 60 fps, so 90/120 Hz games do not flood it
- constant bitrate with frame dropping when the phone supports it, so busy scenes do not cause big frames that queue up
- if encoding falls behind for a second the phone lowers the bitrate a little and raises it again when it catches up
- a frame that is already more than 60 ms old is not sent; the phone skips to a fresh keyframe instead, so delay never piles up
- the PC skips the H.264 loop filter only while decoding falls behind, and `-v` now shows decode CPU time

### Click and drag

- drags, swipes and long press from the PC now work: the touch is sent as one continuous gesture, piece by piece, each piece starting where the last one ended
- mouse moves while the button is held are sent steadily, and the last point is always sent before the release

### USB

- `krapy connect --usb` only asks you to unlock when the lock screen is really showing. It reads several signals and, if they disagree, goes ahead. It never waits more than 20 s

On a loopback test (60 fps 720p clip, send to decoded) the median is about 4 to 5 ms over UDP and TCP. Real glass-to-glass time depends on the phone encoder and the Wi-Fi.
